# Session: Profitability

## How to Use This Pack

Approach each exercise as a real interview - structure quickly, prioritise effectively & communicate clearly.

**For each case:**

- Set a 10–15 minute timer
- Speak your answer out loud
- Apply the 10-Step Profitability Framework
- Check solution only after finishing

## 10-Step Profitability Framework

- **Step 1: Check External vs Internal Drivers**
  - Determine if the issue is industry-wide or company-specific
- **Step 2: Assess External Factors**
  - Review: Macroeconomic conditions + Industry dynamics
- **Step 3: Analyse Company-Specific Drivers**
  - Identify whether the issue is: revenue-driven, cost-driven or both
- **Step 4: Segment Revenue to Find the Signal**
  - Break revenue into key components
  - Identify where the issue is concentrated
- **Step 5: Quantify & Isolate the Root Cause (Revenue)**
  - Use data and process of elimination
  - Focus on the largest driver
- **Step 6: Refine Hypothesis**
  - Update your hypothesis based on insights
  - Redirect analysis if needed
- **Step 7: Segment Costs to Find the Signal**
  - Break costs into meaningful categories
  - Identify key cost drivers
- **Step 8: Quantify & Isolate the Root Cause (Costs)**
  - Confirm impact using quantitative analysis
- **Step 9: Go Beyond the Numbers**
  - Use qualitative analysis to understand why. Consider:
    - Competition
    - Customer behaviour
    - Company capabilities
- **Step 10: Recommendation**
  - Clear recommendation
  - Supporting reasons
  - Risks
  - Next steps

## CASE 1 Clothing Retailer
**Difficulty: Very Easy**

**Prompt:** A clothing retailer has seen a 15% decline in profits over the past year. You have been asked to diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors' profits are stable (which means this is a company-specific issue)
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Is this driven by revenue or costs?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue ↓ 12% and Costs remain the same
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment revenue into price and volume."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Price → flat, Volume ↓ 12%
  - *"I would like to segment volume further."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Online → stable, Physical stores ↓ 20%
- **Quantify & Isolate:** *"It appears the main driver is…"*
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Shift to online shopping + Poor in-store experience
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "My initial hypothesis is that the profit decline could be driven by either a drop in revenue or an increase in costs. I will test both."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"
  - Since competitors are stable → not macro / industry. This suggests: Company-specific issue.
  - "Since competitors are stable, I believe this is a company-specific issue rather than an industry-wide problem."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "I'd like to determine whether this is revenue- or cost-driven."
  - Revenue is the driver (Revenue ↓ 12%). Cost stays the same.
  - "It appears the decline is revenue-driven, so I will focus on revenue."
- **Segmentation:** "I would like to segment revenue into price and volume."
  - Price stable (Price → flat) → not pricing issue
  - Volume decline (Volume ↓ 12%) → demand / traffic issue
  - "Since price is stable, the issue is likely driven by a decline in volume."
- **Segmentation:** "I would like to segment volume into its component parts to identify where the decline is coming from."
  - Problem is not everywhere (Online → stable)
  - It is concentrated in stores (Physical stores ↓ 20%)
  - This is critical - you've isolated the signal
  - "Decline appears to be concentrated in physical stores, while online performance stays stable."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "It appears the primary driver of the profit decline is reduced store traffic."
  - You have identified where the issue is + quantified its impact
  - This is your root cause (quantitative)
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I'd like to explore why store traffic has declined."
  - Shift to online shopping
  - Poor in-store experience
  - This explains: Why stores ↓ + Why online stable
- **Recommendation:** Strengthen client's omnichannel strategy while improving the in-store experience
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is primarily driven by reduced store traffic
  - The issue is localized to physical stores, not online
  - Customer behaviour is shifting toward online channels
- **Risks:**
  - Investment cost in store transformation
  - Execution risk
  - Potential lag before results materialise
- **Next Steps:**
  - Analyse customer journey across channels
  - Pilot store redesign initiatives
  - Strengthen integration between online and offline

    - **DATA (only after asking):** Bangkok revenue ↓ 20%, Other regions stable
- **Quantify & Isolate:** *"It appears the main driver is…"*
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** New low-cost competitor entered Bangkok; Competitor offering aggressive promotions; Client responded with discounts
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "My initial hypothesis is that profit decline may be driven by lower revenue in specific locations or higher local operating costs. I will test both."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors' profits also declined?"
  - Competitors are also seeing pressure, but only in Bangkok.
  - This suggests a local market issue, not a full company-wide issue.
  - "This appears to be a market-specific issue, concentrated in Bangkok."

  - Revenue ↓ 12%. Costs stable.
  - This is primarily revenue-driven.
  - "Since costs are stable, I will focus on revenue."

  - Average price ↓ 8%
  - Customer volume ↓ 4% (Both matter, but price is the larger driver.)
  - "Revenue decline is driven mainly by lower price, with volume decline as a secondary factor."

  - New low-cost competitor entered Bangkok
  - Competitor offering aggressive promotions
  - Client responded with discounts
  - This is a price war / competitive positioning issue.
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend reducing reliance on discounting and strengthening differentiation in Bangkok."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is primarily driven by price reductions
  - The issue is concentrated in Bangkok
  - New low-cost competitor is creating price pressure
- **Risks:**
  - Raising prices may reduce volume
  - Differentiation may take time
  - Competitor may continue aggressive discounting
- **Next Steps:**
  - Analyse customer willingness to pay
  - Test bundled offers instead of broad discounts
  - Review competitor positioning and menu strategy

## CASE 3 Mid-market Fashion Retailer
**Difficulty: Easy**

**Prompt:** The client is a mid-sized fashion retailer offering basic everyday apparel and a premium fashion line. They sell through physical stores and online channels. They have seen a 10% decline in profits over the past year, despite stable operating costs. You have been asked to diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors' profits are stable
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Is this driven by revenue or costs?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue ↓ 10%; Costs stable
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment revenue into price and volume."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Average price ↓ 5%, Volume ↓ 5%
  - *"I would like to segment revenue by location to see where the issue is concentrated."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Segment | Price Change | Volume Change |
      |---|---|---|
      | Basic apparel | Stable | Stable |
      | Premium line | ↓ 10% | ↓ 8% |

- **Quantify & Isolate:** *"It appears the main driver is…"*
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors launched similar premium products at lower prices; Customers perceive limited differentiation; Brand positioning unclear in premium space
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "My initial hypothesis is that the decline in profit is likely driven by a reduction in revenue, since costs are stable. I would like to test whether this is due to changes in price, volume, or both."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors also experienced a decline in profits?"
  - Competitors' profits are stable. Economy fine + not industry-wide issue, so company-specific issue.
  - "Since competitors are stable, this appears to be a company-specific issue. I will focus on the client's internal drivers."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we confirm whether this is driven by revenue or costs?"
  - Revenue ↓ 10%, Costs → stable
  - Entire profit decline = revenue issue. Focus ONLY on revenue.
  - "Since costs are stable, I will focus on revenue."
- **Segmentation:** "I would like to segment revenue into price and volume."
  - Average price ↓ 5%, Volume ↓ 5% (BOTH drivers matter)
  - Need to go deeper → where is this happening?
  - "Both price & volume contribute to the decline, so I'd like to understand where this is happening."
- **Segment by Product Line:** "I would like to segment performance by product category."
  - Basic → no issue. Premium → BOTH price & volume down
  - "The decline is concentrated in the premium product line, where both price and volume have decreased significantly."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "It appears the profit decline is primarily driven by weaker performance in the premium segment, where both price and volume have declined."
  - Premium segment: Lower price + Lower demand → Drives majority of profit decline
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I would like to understand why the premium segment is underperforming."
  - Competitors launched similar premium products at lower prices
  - Customers perceive limited differentiation
  - Brand positioning unclear in premium space
  - This is NOT demand issue overall + NOT a cost issue → value perception + positioning problem
  - "It appears that customers do not perceive sufficient value in the premium offering relative to competitors, leading to both lower willingness to pay and reduced demand."
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend that the client reposition and strengthen its premium product offering while selectively reviewing pricing to restore competitiveness."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is primarily driven by the premium segment
  - Both price and volume have declined in that segment
  - Customers perceive lower value relative to competitors
- **Risks:**
  - Lowering price may erode brand perception
  - Product redesign may require investment
  - Competitors may continue aggressive pricing
- **Next Steps:**
  - Conduct customer willingness-to-pay analysis
  - Benchmark competitor premium offerings
  - Redesign premium product positioning
  - Pilot revised pricing strategy

## CASE 4 Consumer Electronics Manufacturer
**Difficulty: Easy**

**Prompt:** The client is a mid-sized consumer electronics manufacturer producing:
- Product A (high-volume, mid-margin)
- Product B (lower volume, higher margin)

They sell globally through distributors. The client's profits have declined by 12% over the past year, despite sales volume increasing by 10%. You have been asked to diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors' profits are stable
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Is this driven by revenue or costs?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue ↑ 10%; Costs ↑ 22%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment costs into fixed and variable costs."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Fixed costs → stable, Variable costs ↑ 25%
  - *"I would like to segment variable costs into their components."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Cost Type | Change |
      |---|---|
      | Labour | +3% |
      | Raw materials | +30% |
      | Shipping | +5% |

  - *"Can we segment raw material cost increases by product line?"*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Product A → raw material cost ↑ 40%, Product B → stable
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Supplier contract expired; New supplier pricing ↑ significantly; Client has limited alternative suppliers; No long-term price lock / hedging
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since sales volume is increasing but profits are declining, my initial hypothesis is that the issue is likely cost-driven, though I will briefly validate revenue before focusing on costs."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors also experienced a decline in profits?"
  - Competitors' profits are stable. Economy fine + not industry-wide issue, so company-specific issue.
  - "Since competitors are stable, this appears to be a company-specific issue. I will focus on the client's internal drivers."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"
  - Revenue ↑ 10%, Costs ↑ 22%
  - Revenue growing → NOT the issue, Costs growing much faster → core problem
  - "Since costs are increasing significantly faster than revenue, I will focus on the cost side as the primary driver."
- **Segmentation:** "I would like to segment costs into key segments such as variable vs. fixed costs."
  - Fixed costs → stable (not the issue), Variable costs ↑ 25% (key driver)
  - "The increase appears to be driven by variable costs, so I would like to break those down further."
- **Segment Variable Costs (2nd level):** "I would like to segment variable costs into their components."
  - Raw materials clearly stand out (based on table)
  - "Raw material costs have increased significantly more than other cost components, so I would focus on this area."
- **Segment by Product (3rd level):** "Can we segment raw material cost increases by product line?"
  - Product A → raw material cost ↑ 40%, Product B → stable
  - Not company-wide, Not category-wide — Very specific issue (Product A)
  - "The cost increase appears to be concentrated in Product A, while Product B remains stable."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "It appears that the profit decline is primarily driven by a significant increase in raw material costs for Product A."
  - Product A raw material cost ↑ significantly — This is the largest contributor to total cost increase
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I would like to understand why raw material costs for Product A have increased."
  - Supplier contract expired
  - New supplier pricing ↑ significantly
  - Client has limited alternative suppliers
  - No long-term price lock / hedging
  - This is NOT random + NOT temporary — It is a procurement / supplier strategy issue
  - "It appears the cost increase is driven by supplier pricing changes, combined with limited supplier diversification and lack of long-term contracting."
- **Make sure MECE:** "Before moving to recommendations, I would like to briefly confirm that no other cost or revenue drivers materially contribute to the issue." — This shows: Prioritisation, Completeness, Senior thinking
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend that the client address the increase in raw material costs for Product A by improving supplier strategy and exploring cost optimisation opportunities."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is driven primarily by cost increases, not revenue issues
  - Variable costs (specifically raw materials) are the largest driver
  - The issue is concentrated in Product A, indicating a targeted solution is possible
- **Risks:**
  - Switching suppliers may impact quality
  - Renegotiation may not fully offset cost increases
  - Product redesign may take time
- **Next Steps:**
  - Launch supplier renegotiation or RFP process
  - Explore alternative suppliers or materials
  - Assess potential price pass-through to customers
  - Evaluate redesign options to reduce material usage

## CASE 5 E-commerce Marketplace Platform
**Difficulty: Intermediate**

**Prompt:** The client is a large e-commerce marketplace platform earning revenue through:
- Commission (take rate) on transactions
- Logistics / fulfilment services

Key categories include: Electronics, Fashion, Beauty.

The client has seen a significant decline in profit margins over the past year, despite stable overall sales (GMV). Diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Yes - competitors are also seeing margin pressure, but less severe than the client
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue per transaction and costs have changed?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** GMV → stable; Take rate (% of transaction value that the platform keeps as revenue) ↓ 2 percentage points; Logistics cost ↑ 12%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment performance by product category."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Category | Margin Change |
      |---|---|
      | Electronics | ↓↓↓ significant |
      | Fashion | Stable |
      | Beauty | Stable |

  - *"I would like to break down electronics performance into take rate and logistics costs."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Take rate ↓ 4 percentage points; Logistics cost per order ↑ 18%
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Intense competition in electronics (price wars); Sellers demanding lower commissions; Electronics products are: Bulkier, More expensive to ship, Higher return rates
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since sales are stable but margins are declining, my initial hypothesis is that the issue is likely driven by lower monetisation (e.g. take rate), higher costs (e.g. logistics), or a shift in category mix."
- **External vs Internal:** "Are competitors experiencing similar margin pressure?"
  - Yes — competitors are also seeing margin pressure, but less severe than the client
  - Industry-wide pressure exists BUT client is underperforming peers — this is both external + internal
  - "This appears to be partly industry-driven, but the client is underperforming competitors, suggesting internal factors are also contributing."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue per transaction and costs have changed?"
  - GMV → stable, Take rate ↓ 2 percentage points, Logistics cost ↑ 12%
  - Revenue per unit ↓, Cost per unit ↑ (Dual driver case)
  - "Margins are being pressured both by declining take rate and increasing logistics costs, so I would like to understand where this is happening."
- **Segment by Category (1st critical step):** "I'd like to segment performance by product category."
  - See table — Not company-wide + Electronics is the problem (this is your first major isolation)
  - "The margin decline appears to be concentrated in the electronics category."
- **Segment Electronics (2nd level):** "I would like to break down electronics performance into take rate and logistics costs."
  - Take rate ↓ 4 percentage points, Logistics cost per order ↑ 18%
  - BOTH revenue and cost are worse in electronics — Electronics = structurally weaker economics
  - "Electronics is affected by both declining take rates and increasing logistics costs."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "It appears that the majority of the margin decline is driven by the electronics category, due to both lower take rates and higher fulfilment costs."
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I'd like to understand why electronics margins are under pressure."
  - Intense competition in electronics (price wars)
  - Sellers demanding lower commissions
  - Electronics products are: Bulkier, More expensive to ship, Higher return rates
  - This is NOT just "cost went up" — it is category economics + competition
  - "Electronics appears to have structurally lower margins due to competitive pressure and inherently higher fulfilment costs."
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend that the client reduce reliance on low-margin electronics growth while improving the economics of that category."
- **Reasons:**
  - Margin decline is concentrated in electronics
  - Electronics suffers from both lower take rate and higher costs
  - Competitive pressure and fulfilment complexity make margins structurally weaker
- **Risks:**
  - Reducing electronics focus may reduce traffic
  - Sellers may shift to competitors
  - Logistics optimisation may take time
- **Next Steps:**
  - Analyse category-level profitability in detail
  - Renegotiate logistics costs
  - Adjust commission structure selectively
  - Shift marketing toward higher-margin categories

## CASE 6 Airline
**Difficulty: Intermediate**

**Prompt:** The client is a regional airline operating domestic routes and international routes (short-haul & long-haul). Revenue comes from ticket sales and ancillary fees (bags, seats). Major cost drivers: Fuel, Labour, Aircraft operations.

The airline's profits have declined by 15% over the past year, despite stable passenger numbers. Diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Yes - most airlines are experiencing some profit pressure
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue per passenger and costs have changed?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Passenger volume → stable; Average ticket price ↓ 7%; Costs ↑ 12%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment ticket prices by route type."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Route Type | Price Change |
      |---|---|
      | Domestic | Stable |
      | International | ↓ 12% |

  - *"I would like to understand how cost increases vary across routes."*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Fuel cost ↑ across all routes; BUT impact is much higher on long-haul international routes
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** New low-cost airlines entering international routes; Increased competition → fare pressure; Fuel prices ↑ globally; Client uses older aircraft (less fuel-efficient)
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since passenger numbers are stable but profits are declining, my initial hypothesis is that the issue is likely driven by lower ticket prices, higher costs, or both."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have other airlines experienced similar profit declines?"
  - Yes - most airlines are experiencing some profit pressure
  - Industry-wide pressure exists BUT we still need to see: is the client worse than peers?
  - "This suggests there are external pressures, but I would still like to understand how the client is performing relative to competitors."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue per passenger and costs have changed?"
  - Passenger volume → stable, Average ticket price ↓ 7%, Costs ↑ 12%
  - This is a dual-driver case — Revenue ↓ (price), Cost ↑
  - "The profit decline appears to be driven by both lower ticket prices and higher costs, so I would like to understand where these changes are occurring."
- **Segment Revenue (1st critical step):** "I would like to segment ticket prices by route type."
  - See table — Revenue issue is NOT everywhere. It is concentrated in international routes.
  - "The decline in ticket prices is concentrated in international routes."
- **Segment Costs (2nd level):** "I would like to understand how cost increases vary across routes."
  - Fuel cost ↑ across all routes; BUT impact is much higher on long-haul international routes
  - Revenue problem → international; Cost problem → also international
  - "International routes are being impacted both by lower ticket prices and higher fuel costs."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "The majority of the profit decline appears to be driven by international routes, where both pricing pressure and fuel costs are impacting margins."
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I'd like to understand why international routes are underperforming."
  - New low-cost airlines entering international routes
  - Increased competition → fare pressure
  - Fuel prices ↑ globally
  - Client uses older aircraft (less fuel-efficient)
  - External (fuel, competition); Internal (fleet inefficiency)
  - "International routes are under pressure due to increased competition lowering fares, combined with higher fuel costs and the client's relatively inefficient fleet."
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend that the client focus on improving profitability in international routes through route optimisation, pricing discipline, and cost efficiency improvements."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is concentrated in international routes
  - These routes face both price pressure and cost increases
  - The client's older aircraft amplify fuel cost impact
- **Risks:**
  - Reducing routes may reduce network competitiveness
  - Increasing prices may reduce demand
  - Fleet upgrades require significant investment
- **Next Steps:**
  - Conduct route-level profitability analysis
  - Review pricing strategy on international routes
  - Evaluate fleet upgrade or fuel efficiency initiatives
  - Consider exiting or reducing unprofitable routes

## CASE 7 Telecom Operator
**Difficulty: Advanced**

**Prompt:** The client is a telecom operator offering prepaid plans and postpaid plans. Revenue comes from: Monthly subscriptions, Usage charges. Costs include: Network infrastructure, Customer acquisition, Customer retention (discounts, promotions).

The client's revenue is flat, but profits have declined by 10% over the past year. Diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** No - Competitors are stable
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue → flat; Costs ↑ 10%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment costs into key categories."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Cost Category | Change |
      |---|---|
      | Network | Stable |
      | Acquisition | +5% |
      | Retention | +25% |

  - *"Can we break retention costs down by customer segment?"*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Prepaid retention costs ↑ 35%; Postpaid → stable
- **Go deeper:** *"I would like to understand why retention costs for prepaid customers are increasing."*
  - Prepaid churn ↑ significantly
  - Competitors offering aggressive promotions
  - Client responding with heavy discounts
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors offering better pricing; Short-term promotional offers; Low switching barriers; No strong customer loyalty
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since revenue is flat but profits are declining, my initial hypothesis is that the issue is likely cost-driven, though I would also consider potential changes in customer mix."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors experienced similar profit declines?"
  - Competitors are stable — Not industry-wide issue (company-specific)
  - "Since competitors are stable, this appears to be a company-specific issue."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"
  - Revenue → flat, Costs ↑ 10%
  - Looks like a cost problem — BUT do NOT stop here (this is the trap)
  - "At first glance, the issue appears cost-driven, so I would like to break down the cost structure."
- **Segment Costs (1st CRITICAL STEP):** "I would like to segment costs into key categories."
  - See table — Retention costs clearly stand out — BUT ask: why are retention costs increasing?
  - "Retention costs appear to be increasing significantly, so I'd like to understand what is driving this."
- **Segment retention costs (2nd level):** "Can we break retention costs down by customer segment?"
  - Prepaid retention costs ↑ 35%, Postpaid → stable
  - Problem is: NOT all customers; Specific to prepaid
  - "The issue appears to be concentrated in prepaid customers."
- **CRITICAL TURNING POINT (THIS IS THE TRAP):**
  - Most candidates stop here: "Retention costs increased → cost problem" — That is wrong / incomplete
- **Go Deeper (ROOT CAUSE DRILL):** "I would like to understand why retention costs for prepaid customers are increasing."
  - Prepaid churn ↑ significantly
  - Competitors offering aggressive promotions
  - Client responding with heavy discounts
  - Retention cost ↑ is NOT the problem (it is a symptom) — The real problem is churn
  - "It appears that the increase in retention costs is actually a response to rising churn in prepaid customers."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "The root cause of the profit decline appears to be increased churn in prepaid customers, which is driving higher retention costs."
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Why is churn increasing in prepaid customers?"
  - Competitors offering better pricing
  - Short-term promotional offers
  - Low switching barriers
  - No strong customer loyalty
  - Structural issue: Weak customer stickiness; Highly price-sensitive segment
  - "Prepaid customers appear highly price-sensitive and are switching to competitors offering better promotions."
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend shifting from broad retention spending to targeted customer retention strategies, while addressing the root cause of prepaid churn."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is driven by increased retention costs
  - Retention costs are rising due to higher churn in prepaid customers
  - The underlying issue is competitive pressure and low customer loyalty
- **Risks:**
  - Reducing discounts may increase churn
  - Targeting may miss some customers
  - Competitors may escalate promotions
- **Next Steps:**
  - Segment prepaid customers by lifetime value
  - Design targeted retention offers
  - Analyse competitor pricing and promotions
  - Explore ways to increase customer stickiness

## CASE 8 Pharmaceutical Company
**Difficulty: Advanced**

**Prompt:** The client is a pharma company with a legacy drug (high margin, mature) and a new drug (recently launched, growing fast). Revenue drivers: Drug sales volume, Drug pricing (regulated / negotiated with payers). Costs include: Manufacturing, Salesforce support, Regulatory / compliance.

After launching a new drug, the client's total revenue has remained stable, but profits have declined by 12%. Diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*

- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"*

- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment costs into key categories."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Product | Revenue Change | Margin |
      |---|---|---|
      | Legacy drug | ↓ | 60% |
      | New drug | ↑ | 30% |

  - *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."* (Why section)
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Higher manufacturing cost; Lower pricing (payer pressure); Higher salesforce / marketing cost; Introductory pricing strategy
- **Go deeper: check CANNIBALISATION**
  - Yes — many customers switched from legacy to new
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since revenue is stable but profits are declining, my initial hypothesis is that the issue is likely driven by margin compression, potentially due to product mix shift or higher costs associated with the new product."
- **External vs Internal:** "Have competitors experienced similar profit declines?"
  - Competitors are stable — Not industry-wide issue (company-specific)
  - "Since competitors are stable, this appears to be a company-specific issue, likely related to the new product launch."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"
  - Total revenue → flat, Costs ↑ slightly, Profit ↓ significantly
  - Revenue alone does NOT explain the issue — Need to go deeper → margin / mix
  - "Since revenue is stable but profit has declined, I would like to examine product-level margins and mix."
- **Segment Costs (1st CRITICAL STEP):** "I would like to segment revenue and profit by product."
  - See table — High-margin product ↓, Low-margin product ↑ — This is a mix shift problem
  - "It appears that the new product is replacing the legacy product, but at a significantly lower margin."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "The profit decline appears to be driven by a shift in product mix from the high-margin legacy drug to the lower-margin new drug."
  - What you have done — Identified: WHERE → product level; WHAT → margin difference; WHY profit ↓ despite stable revenue
- **Why (Qualitative):** "I would like to understand why the new product has lower margins."

  - Lower pricing (payer pressure)
  - Higher salesforce / marketing cost

- **Risks:**
  - Limiting new product growth may hurt long-term positioning
  - Increasing price may reduce demand
  - Protecting legacy product may conflict with innovation strategy
- **Next Steps:**
  - Conduct customer segmentation to understand switching behaviour
  - Reassess pricing strategy for new product
  - Explore cost reduction opportunities
  - Evaluate positioning of both products

## CASE 9 National Supermarket Chain
**Difficulty: Advanced**

**Prompt:** The client is a national supermarket chain operating: Urban stores, Suburban stores. They compete on: Price, Convenience, Location.

The client's profits have declined by 12%, while competitors are growing profits by ~5%. Diagnose the issue and recommend actions.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors' profits ↑ 5%
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue ↓ 8%; Costs ↑ 2%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment revenue to understand where the decline is coming from."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Store Type | Revenue Change |
      |---|---|
      | Urban | Stable |
      | Suburban | ↓ 18% |

  - *"Can we break suburban revenue into price and volume?"*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** Price → stable; Customer traffic ↓ 18%
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors opened stores closer to residential areas; Client stores have worse parking / accessibility; Competitors have stronger delivery offering; Client has weaker online-to-offline integration
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since competitors are performing well while the client is declining, my initial hypothesis is that this is a company-specific issue, likely driven by revenue decline or store-level underperformance."
- **External vs Internal:** "Can we confirm whether competitors are facing similar trends?"
  - Competitors' profits ↑ 5% — Not industry-wide issue (company-specific)
  - "Since competitors are growing while the client is declining, this is clearly a company-specific issue."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"
  - Revenue ↓ 8%, Costs ↑ 2%
  - Revenue is main driver, costs secondary
  - "The profit decline appears primarily driven by a reduction in revenue."
- **Segment Revenue (1st CRITICAL STEP):** "I would like to segment revenue to understand where the decline is coming from."
  - See table — Not company-wide, Not random — VERY CLEAR: Suburban stores = problem
  - "The decline appears to be concentrated in suburban stores."
- **Segment Further (2nd CRITICAL STEP):** "Can we break suburban revenue into price and volume?"
  - Price → stable, Customer traffic ↓ 18%
  - NOT pricing, NOT basket size — It is a traffic problem
  - "The issue is driven by declining customer traffic rather than pricing."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "The profit decline is primarily driven by reduced traffic in suburban stores."
  - What you have done: WHERE → suburban; WHAT → traffic (your core answer)
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I would like to understand why traffic has declined in suburban stores."
  - Competitors opened stores closer to residential areas
  - Client stores have worse parking / accessibility
  - Competitors have stronger delivery offering
  - Client has weaker online-to-offline integration
  - This is NOT: pricing issue + product issue — It is: convenience + accessibility problem
  - "It appears that the client is losing traffic due to a convenience disadvantage relative to competitors (customers choosing competitors due to better convenience)"
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend improving competitiveness in suburban markets by addressing convenience gaps through store optimisation and enhanced delivery capabilities."
- **Reasons:**
  - Profit decline is driven by revenue decline, not cost
  - Revenue decline is concentrated in suburban stores
  - Traffic loss is driven by convenience disadvantages vs competitors
- **Risks:**
  - Store relocation or redesign is costly
  - Delivery expansion may reduce margins
  - Competitors may respond quickly
- **Next Steps:**
  - Conduct store-level profitability and location analysis
  - Benchmark competitor store proximity
  - Improve delivery coverage and speed
  - Pilot improvements in key suburban areas

## CASE 10 B2B SaaS Company
**Difficulty: Advanced**

**Prompt:** The client is a B2B SaaS company serving enterprise clients (large corporations) & SMB clients (small & medium businesses). Revenue model is subscription-based (monthly / annual). Key cost drivers: Customer acquisition, Customer support, Product development.

*SaaS (Software as a Service) is software that users access online (via the cloud) and pay for on a subscription basis.*

The client's revenue has grown by 20%, but profits have declined by 10%. Some segments are highly profitable, while others are not. Diagnose the issue and recommend a path forward.

### Your Answer

- **Hypothesis:** *"My initial hypothesis is…"*
- **External vs Internal:** *"Have competitors also seen a decline in profits?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Competitors focused on enterprise remain profitable; Competitors focused on SMB are struggling
- **Revenue vs Cost:** *"Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** Revenue ↑ 20%; Costs ↑ 35%
- **Segmentation:**
  - *"I would like to segment performance by customer segment."*
    - **TABLE (only after asking):**

      | Segment | Revenue Growth | Margin |
      |---|---|---|
      | Enterprise | +15% | +35% |
      | Mid-market | +10% | +15% |
      | SMB | +40% | -10% |

  - *"Can we understand cost structure by segment?"*
    - **DATA (only after asking):** SMB support cost per customer = 3x enterprise; SMB churn = high, SMB pricing = low
- **Why (Qualitative):** *"Now I'd like to understand why this is happening."*
  - **DATA (only after asking):** High onboarding / support needs; Product too complex for SMB → high service cost; High churn → low lifetime value; Sales incentives focused on growth, not profitability
- **Recommendation** (Recommendation, 3 Reasons, Risks, Next Steps)

### Model Answer (only after finishing)

- **Hypothesis:** "Since revenue is growing but profits are declining, my initial hypothesis is that growth is coming from lower-margin or loss-making segments. I would like to segment performance to identify where this is happening."
- **External vs Internal:** "Are competitors experiencing similar profit declines?"
  - Competitors focused on enterprise remain profitable; Competitors focused on SMB are struggling
  - Market difference by segment (not purely company-wide)
  - "This suggests the issue may be related to the client's exposure to different customer segments."
- **Revenue vs Cost:** "Can we understand how revenue and costs have changed?"
  - Revenue ↑ 20%, Costs ↑ 35%
  - Growth is unprofitable (need to understand where growth is coming from)
  - "Since costs are growing faster than revenue, I would like to understand which segments are driving this."
- **Segment by Customer Segment (1st CRITICAL STEP):** "I would like to segment performance by customer segment."
  - See table — SMB → fastest growth, BUT → losing money
  - "The SMB segment is growing rapidly but is loss-making, which appears to be driving the overall profit decline."
- **Segment Costs (2nd CRITICAL STEP):** "Can we understand cost structure by segment?"
  - SMB support cost per customer = 3x enterprise; SMB churn = high; SMB pricing = low
  - SMB = bad unit economics (High cost, Low revenue, High churn)
  - "The SMB segment appears to have poor unit economics, with high servicing costs and low pricing."
- **Quantify & Isolate:** "The primary driver of the profit decline appears to be the SMB segment, which is growing rapidly but is loss-making."
  - WHERE → SMB; WHAT → loss-making; WHY → unit economics
- **Why (Qualitative):** "Now that we've identified the issue quantitatively, I would like to understand why SMB unit economics are so weak."
  - High onboarding / support needs
  - Product too complex for SMB → high service cost
  - High churn → low lifetime value
  - Sales incentives focused on growth, not profitability
  - This is NOT just a pricing issue — It is business model + operating model problem
  - "SMB segment appears structurally unprofitable due to high cost-to-serve, low pricing, high churn."
- **Recognise Strategic Options:** At this point, you should NOT jump straight to 1 answer. You should recognise strategic options. "There are a few strategic paths the client could consider:"
  - Option 1 — Exit SMB
  - Option 2 — Fix SMB economics
  - Option 3 — Focus on enterprise
  - Option 4 — Redesign SMB offering (self-serve)
  - Pick ONE primary + ONE secondary option: Keeps upside (enterprise); Fixes problem (SMB)
- **Recommendation:** "I recommend that the client refocus growth on the enterprise segment while restructuring the SMB model before continuing to scale it."
- **Reasons:**
  - SMB is growing rapidly but is loss-making
  - Poor unit economics (high cost, low pricing, high churn)
  - Enterprise segment remains highly profitable
- **Risks:**
  - Slowing SMB growth may impact market share
  - Exiting SMB could reduce long-term opportunity
  - Restructuring may take time and investment
- **Next Steps:**
  - Conduct detailed unit economics analysis (LTV/CAC)
  - Test SMB self-serve model
  - Adjust pricing and service model
  - Rebalance incentives toward profitable growth
