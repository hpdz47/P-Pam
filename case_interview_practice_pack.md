## Case 1 — Coffee Chain Expansion

**Difficulty: Easy**

A Thai coffee chain is considering entering Cambodia. Should they enter?

The client currently operates 300 stores across Thailand and is known for affordable premium coffee targeted at urban middle-class consumers.

The CEO believes Cambodia may represent an attractive growth opportunity due to rising urbanisation and increasing café culture.

Client has asked you to evaluate whether entering Cambodia would make strategic and financial sense.

### Data Provided To Candidate

**Cambodia Coffee Market**
- Total annual coffee market size = THB 12B
- Premium café segment = 20% of total market
- Expected annual premium café growth = 12%
- Phnom Penh accounts for 60% of premium café demand

**Competition**
- Market fragmented
- Largest competitor market share = 15%
- Average operating margin in industry = 15%

**Client Assumptions**
- Expected achievable market share after 5 years = 5%
- Expected annual revenue per store = THB 15M
- Estimated upfront investment per store = THB 10M

**Management objective**
- Management target revenue = THB 100M within 5 years
- Target payback = <7 years

### Guided Answers

**Strong Structure**

1. Market attractiveness
   - Size of coffee market
   - Growth in café culture
   - Urban middle-class growth
   - Competition intensity
2. Client capabilities
   - Brand strength
   - Operational expertise
   - Supply chain
   - Regional experience
3. Entry strategy
   - Flagship stores
   - Partnerships
   - Franchise
4. Risks & financials
   - Competition
   - Customer adoption
   - Profitability

**Quantitative Analysis Walkthrough**

Numbers candidate should use from prompt:
- Cambodia coffee market = THB 12B annually
- Premium café segment = 20%
- Expected annual market growth = 12%
- Target market share after 5 years = 5%
- Expected operating margin = 15%

Estimated annual revenue opportunity:
Revenue = Total Market Size × % Target Market × % Expected Market Share
= THB 12B × 20% × 5%
≈ THB 120M annual revenue potential

Estimated operating profit:
Operating Profit = Revenue × Operating Margin
= 120M × 15%
≈ THB 18M annually

Stores required: 120M ÷ 15M = 8 stores

Investment: 8 × 10M = THB 80M

Payback: 80M ÷ 18M = 4.4 years (which is <7 years)

**Qualitative Analysis Walkthrough**

Positive signs:
- Growing urban middle class
- Increasing café culture
- Tourism growth
- Relatively fragmented competition

Risks:
- Lower purchasing power than Thailand
- Premium pricing may be challenging
- Local competitors may understand customers better

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would recommend entering Cambodia through a phased rollout focused initially on Phnom Penh.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, the market appears attractive, with strong urbanisation trends and growing café demand.
Second, the client possesses strong operational expertise and a proven coffee shop model that should transfer well to Cambodia.
Third, the opportunity appears financially attractive (meets the payback period requirement), with healthy industry margins and a meaningful revenue opportunity.

*Restate Recommendation*
Therefore, I would recommend entering the market, but through a limited pilot before broader expansion.

*Risks*
- Lower purchasing power than expected
- Slower customer adoption
- Competitive responses from local players

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Launch a small pilot
- Test pricing and customer demand
- Expand only after validating unit economics

## Case 4 — EV Charging Expansion

**Difficulty: Intermediate**

A ride-hailing company is considering launching EV charging stations across Thailand. Should they enter?

The client currently operates one of Thailand's largest ride-hailing platforms with over 300,000 active drivers nationwide.

Management believes EV adoption could accelerate significantly over the next decade and wants to determine whether entering EV charging infrastructure would be attractive.

### Data Provided To Candidate

**Market Information**
- Thailand EV population today = 500,000 vehicles
- Expected annual EV growth = 35%
- Existing charging infrastructure utilisation = 75%

**Economics**
- Each charging station costs THB 2M to build
- Annual fixed operating cost per station = THB 300k
- Average charging fee = THB 250 per charge
- Variable cost per charge = THB 100

**Client Estimates**
- Expected annual charging volume per station = 3,000 charges
- Initial rollout plan = 100 stations

**Management objective**
- Payback within 8 years
- Minimum ROI = 15%

### Guided Answers

**Quantitative Analysis Example**

Calculation 1 — Contribution Margin Per Charge
Contribution Margin = Price − Variable Cost = 250 − 100 = THB 150

Calculation 2 — Breakeven Charging Volume
Breakeven Volume = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution Margin = 300,000 ÷ 150 = 2,000 charges annually

Calculation 3 — Annual Contribution Profit
Contribution Profit = Contribution Margin × Charging Volume = 150 × 3,000 = THB 450,000

Calculation 4 — Annual Operating Profit
Operating Profit = Contribution Profit − Fixed Cost = 450,000 − 300,000 = THB 150,000

Calculation 5 — Payback Period
Payback = Investment ÷ Annual Profit = 2,000,000 ÷ 150,000 = 13.3 years

Calculation 6 — ROI
ROI = Annual Profit ÷ Investment = 150,000 ÷ 2,000,000 = 7.5%

Calculation 7 — Economics Of Full Rollout
Initial rollout: 100 stations
Investment = 100 × 2M = THB 200M
Annual Profit = 100 × 150,000 = THB 15M
Portfolio ROI = 15M ÷ 200M = 7.5% (Same result)

Calculation 8 — Required Volume To Meet ROI Target
Management requires: 15% ROI
Investment = 2M
Required Profit = 2M × 15% = THB 300,000
Required Contribution Profit = Fixed Cost + Required Profit = 300,000 + 300,000 = THB 600,000
Required Charges = 600,000 ÷ 150 = 4,000 charges
Need 33% higher utilisation

Candidates should then ask:
- Is this utilisation realistic?
- How quickly will EV adoption grow?
- What happens if utilisation is only 50% of forecast?

**Strong candidates should identify:**

Positive:
- EV adoption likely growing structurally
- Existing driver ecosystem creates strategic fit
- Early mover advantages possible

Risks:
- Huge capital investment
- Uncertain utilisation rates initially
- Charging technology may evolve rapidly

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would recommend launching a limited pilot of EV charging stations focused on high-density urban areas rather than proceeding with a nationwide rollout.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, Thailand's EV population is expected to grow rapidly, creating a potentially attractive long-term opportunity.
Second, the client has a strong strategic fit given its large existing driver ecosystem and nationwide presence.
Third, while the stations are profitable, the projected ROI of 7.5% and payback period of 13.3 years do not currently meet management's investment criteria.

*Restate Recommendation*
Therefore, I would recommend validating utilisation assumptions through a pilot before committing to broader expansion.

*Risks*
- Lower-than-expected utilisation
- Slower EV adoption
- Technology changes

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Launch pilot stations in high-density locations
- Track utilisation and profitability closely
- Refine rollout economics before scaling

## Case 9 — Low-Cost Airline

**Difficulty: Advanced**

A premium airline is considering launching a low-cost subsidiary. Should they proceed?

Management believes the low-cost travel segment is growing rapidly but is concerned about possible cannibalisation of its premium business.

### Data Provided To Candidate

**Current Premium Business**
- 1M passengers annually
- THB 4,000 profit per passenger

**Proposed Low-Cost Airline**
- Expected new passengers = 500k annually
- Expected profit per passenger = THB 1,000

**Risk Assumption**
- 20% of premium passengers may switch to low-cost offering

### Guided Answers

**Critical Insight**

Strong candidates should identify potential problem: The new low-cost airline could *cannibalise* the premium business.

This becomes a key decision-driving issue.

**Quantitative Analysis Example**

Cannibalised passengers: 1M × 20% = 200k passengers
Profit lost: 200k × THB 4,000 = THB 800M

Profit gained from new low-cost passengers: 500k × THB 1,000 = THB 500M

Net Profit Impact: 500M − 800M = −THB 300M
*Under current assumptions, launching the low-cost airline would reduce overall profitability*

**Strong Trade-Off Analysis**

| Potential Benefit | Potential Risk |
|---|---|
| Broader customer reach | Brand dilution |
| Higher growth | Cannibalisation |
| Competitive defence | Operational complexity |

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would not recommend launching the low-cost airline under the current assumptions.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, while the low-cost travel segment is growing rapidly and offers access to a broader customer base, the financial impact appears unattractive.
Second, the client already possesses strong aviation capabilities and could potentially compete successfully in the segment.
Third, the projected cannibalisation of the premium business outweighs the expected profit from new low-cost customers, resulting in an estimated net profit decline of approximately THB 300M annually.

*Restate Recommendation*
I would not recommend proceeding with a full-scale launch unless management can significantly reduce cannibalisation risk or improve low-cost economics.

*Risks*
- Underestimating market growth
- Competitors capturing the low-cost segment
- Loss of future market share

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Pilot selected routes before a full launch
- Create clear customer segmentation between premium and low-cost brands
- Test whether the low-cost offering attracts genuinely new customers rather than existing premium customers

## Case 11 — Rural Food Delivery

**Difficulty: Advanced**

A food delivery platform wants to expand into rural areas. Should they proceed?

Current urban order volume = 100M annually
Management believes rural expansion can add 10M orders annually

**Urban economics:**
- Average delivery fee = THB 80
- Average delivery cost = THB 50
- Contribution profit = THB 30

**Rural economics:**
- Average delivery fee = THB 80
- Average delivery cost = THB 110
- Contribution profit = negative THB 30

### Guided Answers

**Critical Insight**

Strong candidates should quickly identify: The key issue may NOT be demand.

It may instead be:
- Delivery economics
- Driver density
- Operational efficiency

This is a classic prioritisation test.

**Quantitative Analysis Example**

Strong candidates should identify: Expansion may increase order volume but worsen overall profitability if delivery density remains too low.

Calculation 1 — Urban Contribution Margin Per Order
Urban Contribution Profit = Delivery Fee − Delivery Cost = 80 − 50 = THB 30 per order
*Each urban order creates positive contribution profit.*

Calculation 2 — Rural Contribution Margin Per Order
Rural Contribution Profit = Delivery Fee − Delivery Cost = 80 − 110 = −THB 30 per order
*Each rural order destroys value.*

Calculation 3 — Total Annual Contribution Impact
Management forecast = 10M annual rural orders
Total Contribution = 10M × (−30) = −THB 300M annually
*Expansion would reduce profitability by THB 300M per year under current assumptions.*

Calculation 4 — Breakeven Delivery Fee
Current Delivery Cost = THB 110
Current Delivery Fee = THB 80
Required Fee = THB 110
Additional Fee Required = 110 − 80 = THB 30 increase
*Prices would need to increase by approximately 38% just to break even*

Calculation 5 — Required Cost Reduction To Break Even
Current Cost = THB 110
Target Cost = THB 80
Required Reduction = 110 − 80 = THB 30 per order
Percentage Reduction = 30 ÷ 110 ≈ 27%
*Delivery costs would need to fall by approximately 27% to achieve breakeven economics.*

Calculation 6 — Economics If Delivery Density Improves
Assume batching and route optimisation reduce delivery cost to THB 90.
New Contribution Profit = 80 − 90 = −THB 10 per order
Annual Impact = 10M × (−10) = −THB 100M
*Even significant efficiency improvements may not fully solve the economics problem.*

**Strong candidates should conclude:**

Positive:
- Demand likely exists
- Rural customers are underserved

Negative:
- Current unit economics are negative
- Expansion destroys approximately THB 300M annually
- Breakeven requires either: 38% higher pricing or 27% lower costs
- Therefore the key issue is economics, not demand

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would not recommend broad rural expansion at this time.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, while rural demand exists, delivery economics are significantly less attractive than in urban markets.
Second, low delivery density increases driver costs and reduces operational efficiency.
Third, current contribution margins are negative, meaning expansion would likely destroy value rather than create it.

*Restate Recommendation*
Therefore, I would recommend pursuing only highly selective pilots until a sustainable operating model can be identified.

*Risks*
- Continued negative unit economics
- Low driver density
- Operational complexity

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Pilot selected rural regions
- Explore delivery batching and alternative fulfilment models
- Test higher pricing or subscription offerings

## Case 14 — Sports Broadcasting

**Difficulty: Advanced**

A streaming platform is considering entering live sports broadcasting. Should they proceed?

- Annual sports rights cost = THB 3B
- Expected new subscribers = 1M
- Monthly subscription fee = THB 250
- Operating margin = 20%
- Current subscribers = 5M
- Current churn = 20%
- Sports expected to reduce churn to 15%

### Guided Answers

**Strong Candidate Thinking**

Candidates should investigate:
- Sports rights costs
- Subscriber acquisition impact
- Retention effects
- Competitive positioning
- Monetisation

Strong candidates should ask:
Is sports content a profitable business itself, or primarily a customer acquisition and retention tool? This is a VERY important strategic distinction.

**Calculations required:**

- Annual revenue = 1M × THB 250 × 12 = THB 3B
- Operating profit before rights cost = THB 3B × 20% = THB 600M
- Net impact after rights cost = THB 600M − THB 3B = −THB 2.4B
- Retained subscribers = 5M × (20%−15%) = 250,000 subscribers retained
  - Without Sports: Subscribers lost: 5M × 20% = 1M subscribers
  - With Sports: Subscribers lost: 5M × 15% = 750k subscribers
  - Subscribers Saved: 1M − 750k = 250k subscribers OR 5M × (20% − 15%) = 250k subscribers
  - *Sports rights are not attractive as a standalone profit pool; they only make sense if they materially improve retention, brand differentiation, or long-term subscriber lifetime value.*

Sports rights lose THB 2.4B directly. Is retaining 250k subscribers worth that cost?

Estimate subscriber lifetime value (LTV).
- Annual revenue per subscriber: 250 × 12 = THB 3,000
- 250k subscribers retained:
- 250k × 3,000 = THB 750M annual revenue preserved
  - Direct economics are unattractive, but sports may still make sense if retention, LTV, and competitive positioning benefits are large enough.

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would not recommend acquiring sports rights solely as a profit-generating opportunity.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, sports content may help differentiate the platform and attract subscribers.
Second, sports rights can improve customer retention and strengthen competitive positioning.
Third, however, the direct economics appear unattractive, with sports rights costs significantly exceeding expected operating profit.

*Restate Recommendation*
Therefore, I would recommend a highly selective sports strategy focused on strategic value rather than direct profitability.

*Risks*
- Escalating sports rights costs
- Lower-than-expected subscriber growth
- Competitive bidding pressure

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Prioritise the most valuable sports properties
- Test subscriber acquisition and retention impact
- Regularly reassess economics before expanding commitments

## Case 15 — Metaverse Commerce

**Difficulty: Advanced**

A global luxury fashion brand is considering investing in metaverse commerce and digital fashion assets.

Management believes virtual fashion could become an important future growth channel and wants to determine whether the company should make a significant investment today.

Should the client proceed?

### Data Provided To Candidate

**Market Information — Global Digital Fashion Market**
- Current market size = USD 500M
- Expected CAGR = 40%
- Expected Year 5 market size ≈ USD 2.7B

**Customer Trends**
- 30% of Gen Z consumers have purchased virtual goods
- 60% of luxury consumers under 30 engage with virtual experiences
- Luxury competitors are beginning to experiment with digital assets

**Management Assumptions**
- Target market share after 5 years = 5%
- Average operating margin = 30%
- Initial investment required = USD 100M
- For simplicity, assume Year 5 revenue and profit levels are representative of steady-state performance

**Management Objectives**
- Minimum ROI = 20%
- Maximum acceptable payback = 5 years
- Maintain luxury brand positioning

**Alternative Investment**
Management could instead invest the USD 100M into expanding physical stores.

Expected return:
- ROI physical stores = 25%
- Payback physical stores = 4 years

### Guided Answers

Strong candidates should recognise: this is highly uncertain.

The case is less about precise numbers and more about:
- Strategic optionality
- Brand positioning
- Innovation leadership
- Long-term trend assessment

Strong candidates should avoid:
- Pretending certainty exists
- Overestimating adoption
- Giving overly aggressive rollout recommendations

**Scenario Analysis**

| Scenario | Probability | Year 5 Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Success | 20% | USD 1B |
| Moderate Success | 40% | USD 250M |
| Failure | 40% | USD 0 |

Candidates should discuss:
- Portfolio-style strategic investing
- Optionality value
- Reputational upside
- Whether a smaller experimental investment may generate similar learning at lower risk

**Calculation 6 — Compare Against Alternative Investment**

Alternative:
- Physical stores ROI = 25%
- Physical stores payback = 4 years

| Metric | Metaverse | Physical Stores |
|---|---|---|
| ROI | 40.5% | 25% |
| Payback | 2.5 years | 4 years |

On a purely financial basis, the metaverse investment appears more attractive. However, the uncertainty is significantly higher.

**Calculation 7 — Expected Value Revenue**

Expected Revenue:
= (20% × 1B) + (40% × 250M) + (40% × 0)
= 200M + 100M
= USD 300M

**Calculation 8 — Expected Value Profit**

Expected Profit = 300M × 30% = USD 90M

**Calculation 9 — Expected ROI**

Expected ROI = 90M ÷ 100M = 90%

**Offer-Level Synthesis**

The opportunity appears attractive financially, with a projected ROI of 40.5% and payback of 2.5 years, both comfortably exceeding management's targets. The economics also compare favourably against the alternative physical store investment. However, the opportunity remains highly uncertain, with outcomes ranging from complete failure to significant upside. Therefore, management should view this as a strategic option and pursue a limited investment that preserves upside while controlling downside risk.

**Strong candidates should identify:**

Positives:
- Attractive market growth
- Strong ROI
- Fast payback
- Strategic learning opportunity
- Brand innovation benefits

Risks:
- Adoption uncertainty
- Monetisation uncertainty
- Technology evolution
- Reputational risk
- Brand dilution

**Critical Insight**

The decision should NOT be: "Will the metaverse definitely succeed?"

The decision should be: "Does the potential upside justify a relatively small investment today given the strategic learning and optionality it creates?"

**Example Recommendation**

*Recommendation*
I would recommend proceeding with a limited investment in metaverse commerce rather than a large-scale commitment.

*Supporting Reasons*
First, the market is expected to grow rapidly and could become an important future channel for luxury consumers.
Second, the opportunity aligns well with the client's premium brand positioning and innovation strategy.
Third, while the expected ROI appears attractive, the investment remains significantly more uncertain than alternative growth opportunities.

*Restate Recommendation*
Therefore, I would recommend treating metaverse commerce as a strategic experiment rather than a core growth engine.

*Risks*
- Lower-than-expected customer adoption
- Technology and platform uncertainty
- Brand dilution risk

*Mitigations / Next Steps*
- Launch limited pilot collections
- Establish clear success metrics
- Increase investment only after demonstrating customer traction
